How to use it

Set up your editions first

  1. Open Settings and answer the four edition questions, the same ones the original Chronological Tolkien calculator asks. Each one asks for a page number from a book's table of contents.
  2. If your edition matches the one this list was built from, the page ranges are exact. Otherwise they’re scaled estimates, marked with “≈”.
  3. To make an estimate close to exact, type in a few chapter start pages under Refine with your table of contents. Two or more per book is enough to bring most ranges within a page or two.
  4. Step 1 of The Silmarillion is split into one step per chapter (chapters 1–19). The app will ask for each chapter’s first page, or for a table from chronology.org, so those pages can be shown accurately. You can also do this later under Settings.
  5. For exact pages in any edition, use Import an exact table. Get your custom page from the calculator on chronology.org, save it or copy its text, and load it here.

Reading in print

  • Work down the Checklist. Each step tells you the book, the pages, and the first and last words of the passage. Every cut falls between paragraphs.
  • If you want to mark your books the classic way, write the two-letter section code (shown on each step) in the margin where it starts, and “Go to” the next code where it ends.
  • The Hobbit is read in whole chapters, so it works with any edition. Its page and time estimates come from the page count or audiobook length you enter in Settings.

Listening to audiobooks

  • Find the chapter track the step names, scrub to roughly the “% of book” position, then listen for the starting words. Stop after the closing words.
  • Audio times are estimates based on page counts at a typical narration pace. Set your playback speed in Settings.
  • Many steps come from the Return of the King appendices and the notes in Unfinished Tales. Check that your recordings include them. Appendix steps name the section, since most recordings give each appendix its own track.

What the estimates mean

  • Chapters: worked out from the page numbers and what each passage covers. A few splits in the middle of a chapter are approximate.
  • % of book: where the passage falls in the printed book. It stays about the same in every edition and in audio.
  • Story dates: rough dates in the reckoning of the Ages (F.A., S.A., T.A., Fo.A.; S.R. is Shire Reckoning). Some passages are essays or notes that span long periods, so they get a broad range.
  • Age headings: the Second Age section starts at the opening of the Akallabêth, and the Third Age section starts right after it ends. The Third Age section also carries the story into the early Fourth Age.

Add it to your iPhone home screen

  1. Open the page in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Open it from the new icon. It runs full screen like an app.

Your checkmarks and settings are saved on this device only. The home-screen app and Safari keep separate copies, so use one or the other, or move your progress with the backup code in Settings.

The opening step of The Silmarillion is split into one step for each of chapters 1–19, so you can see exactly where you are inside it. To show the right pages for your copy, choose one of these:

A. Type each chapter’s first page

Use your book’s contents page. Leave any blank; the grey number is the current estimate.

B. Upload a table from chronology.org

Run the Chronological Tolkien calculator for your editions, then save the result page and choose it here, or paste its text. That table lists pages by section, so it fixes where chapter 1 begins and chapter 19 ends, and the chapters between are spread across that range (close, not exact).
